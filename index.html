Who were the Pandavas?

The Pandavas were the five sons of King Pandu of Hastinapur and the heroes of the Indian epic, the Mahabharata. Their cousins, the hundred Kauravas, claimed the same throne, and the rivalry ended in the great Kurukshetra war.

The Five Brothers

Their mothers were Kunti and Madri, the queens of Pandu. Each brother was born through the blessing of a god.

Yudhishthira

The eldest, known for truth and justice. He became king of Indraprastha and later of Hastinapur.

Bhima

The strongest of the five. He fought with a mace and defeated Duryodhana.

Arjuna

The greatest archer of his time. Krishna taught him the Bhagavad Gita on the battlefield.

Nakula

Handsome and skilled with horses and the sword.

Sahadeva

Nakula’s twin, wise and learned in astrology.

The Story of the Pandavas

The main events of their life, in order.

Childhood in Hastinapur

After Pandu died, Kunti brought the five boys to Hastinapur. They grew up with the Kauravas and trained under Drona.

The house of lac

Duryodhana plotted to burn the Pandavas inside a palace made of lac. They escaped through a secret tunnel.

Draupadi’s swayamvara

Arjuna won the archery contest and Draupadi became the wife of all five brothers.

Indraprastha

The Pandavas turned barren land into the splendid city of Indraprastha. Yudhishthira was crowned king.

The game of dice

In a rigged dice game against Shakuni, Yudhishthira lost his kingdom, his brothers and Draupadi. The Pandavas were sent into exile.

Exile

They lived 12 years in the forest and 1 year in disguise at the court of King Virata.

The Kurukshetra war

Duryodhana refused to return their kingdom, so war began. It lasted 18 days, with Krishna as Arjuna’s charioteer, and the Pandavas won.

The final journey

After ruling for many years, the Pandavas set out for the Himalayas on their last journey.
